How the cost review differs by borrower

What to prepare before comparing costs

  • For salaried borrowers, a clean income trail of recent payslips is usually the core evidence.
  • For contractors and business owners, tax returns, BAS history and a clear explanation of entity structure matter more, because they change which lenders are realistic.
  • For investors, rough rental assumptions for the target property type keep the servicing estimate honest.

Arriving with this pack means the cost discussion covers the whole loan rather than a single headline number.

  1. Define the scenario. Write down the purchase, refinance or investment goal and the single question blocking the next step, including the target suburb and rough purchase range.
  2. Ask how the broker is paid. Ask directly whether payment comes as lender commission, a borrower fee or both, so the cost comparison starts with the funding model in the open.
  3. Test full ownership costs. Add rates, strata and moving costs to the repayment test before judging whether the monthly figure is comfortable.
  4. Check benefits against current guidance. If a first-home grant or duty concession is part of the plan, check it against current Revenue NSW and Housing Australia settings rather than memory.
